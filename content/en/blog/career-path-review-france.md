> **In short:**
> 1. The **career path review** replaces the former professional review. It is mandatory in all private companies.
> 2. It takes place within the year following hiring, then every 4 years, with a summary review every 8 years.
> 3. It covers skills, training and career development, not the assessment of work.
> 4. In companies with at least 50 employees, a failure can lead to a 3,000 euro credit to the personal training account.

## Career path review: definition

The **career path review** (entretien de parcours professionnel, EPP) is a mandatory meeting between the employee and the employer. According to the Service-Public guide checked on 1 October 2026, it replaces the professional review and takes place during working time.

### Employees covered

All employees of private companies are covered, whatever the contract (permanent, fixed-term, apprenticeship) or working time. Temporary agency workers and subcontractors' employees are not covered in the company where they are placed.

## What the review covers

The review covers:

- the employee's skills and qualifications;
- training needs;
- career development wishes;
- the [CPF personal training account]({{< relref "cpf-personal-training-account-france.md" >}});
- career development advice (conseil en évolution professionnelle, CEP).

It does not assess the employee's work, which is the purpose of the annual appraisal.

## The required frequency

| When | Rule |
|---|---|
| After hiring | Review within the year following hiring |
| Regular pace | Every 4 years |
| Return from leave | On return from certain types of leave (maternity, parental, sabbatical, sick leave of more than 6 months) |
| Summary | Summary review of the career path every 8 years |
| Mid-career | Within 2 months of the mid-career medical check-up (age 45) |
| Late career | Within the 2 years before the employee turns 60 |

## Penalties for the employer

- **Companies with at least 50 employees**: a 3,000 euro credit to the employee's CPF if the employee has had neither the reviews nor any training in 8 years.
- **Companies with fewer than 50 employees**: the employee can claim damages if training obligations are not met.

## Putting the review into practice

1. Schedule the first review as soon as the [trial period of a permanent contract]({{< relref "trial-period-permanent-contract-france.md" >}}) ends, in order to meet the one-year deadline.
2. Keep this review clearly separate from the annual appraisal.
3. Keep a written record of each review.
4. Link the discussions to actions taken on [QVCT (quality of life and working conditions)]({{< relref "qvct-quality-of-working-life-definition.md" >}}).

## The summary review every 8 years

The summary review checks that, over the period, the employee has:

- taken at least one training course;
- acquired elements of certification, through training or validation of acquired experience (VAE);
- benefited from a pay rise or career progression.

It results in a written report, a copy of which is given to the employee.

### Frequency and preparation

A collective agreement can set a different frequency for the reviews, provided it does not exceed 4 years. In companies with fewer than 300 employees, employees can rely on career development advice (CEP) to prepare their review.
