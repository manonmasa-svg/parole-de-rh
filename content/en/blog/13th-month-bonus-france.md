> **In brief:**
> 1. The **13th month bonus** and the **year-end bonus** are not required by French law. According to Service-Public, they become mandatory when they are provided for by the employment contract, a collective agreement, a company custom or a unilateral commitment by the employer.
> 2. A year-end bonus paid to all staff, regularly and with a fixed calculation method, can become a company custom. Service-Public gives the example of a bonus paid every year for 5 years.
> 3. Collective agreements differ: a 13th month equal to one month's gross salary in real estate, a 13th instalment calculated pro rata in banking, an annual bonus of €215 to €525 depending on length of service in fast food, and no bonus at all in telecoms.
> 4. The bonus is subject to social security contributions. It does not count when checking that pay meets the minimum wage (Smic), unless it is paid in monthly instalments.

The **13th month bonus** is one of the best-known bonuses in France, but no article of the French Labour Code requires every employer to pay it. Whether it is mandatory, its amount and its conditions all depend on the text or practice that provides for it within the company. This article is based on the Service-Public and Code du travail numérique (the Labour Ministry's online labour code) pages consulted on 24 November 2026.

## 13th month and year-end bonus: two annual gratuities

Service-Public lists the year-end bonus and the 13th month bonus among annual bonuses and gratuities, alongside holiday bonuses and balance sheet bonuses.

These bonuses come on top of the salary. When they are mandatory, they are part of the salary.

The Labour Code sets neither their amount nor their payment conditions. The contract, the collective agreement or the company's practice does. In the real estate collective agreement, for example, the 13th month is equal to one month's gross salary, while the fast food agreement provides for an annual bonus of a fixed amount based on length of service.

## When is the 13th month bonus mandatory?

According to the Service-Public page "Salaire, primes et avantages" (checked on 1 June 2026), paying a bonus or gratuity is mandatory if it is provided for by one of the following:

| Ground | What it means | Can the employer remove it alone? |
|---|---|---|
| Employment contract | The bonus is written in the employee's contract | No, pay cannot be changed without the employee's consent |
| Collective agreement | Industry-wide collective agreement or company agreement | No, the bonus applies as long as the agreement provides for it |
| Company custom | General, constant and fixed practice, not written down | Yes, through a termination procedure |
| Unilateral commitment | Employer's decision to pay the bonus | The pages consulted do not cover this case |

Conversely, a bonus that rests on none of these grounds is not mandatory. Service-Public gives the example of a performance bonus or a variable bonus decided by the employer, which are not part of the salary.

## Company custom: when a habitual bonus becomes a right

A company custom (usage d'entreprise) is a benefit granted freely and repeatedly by the employer, without the law, the collective agreement or the contract requiring it. According to Service-Public (page checked on 22 April 2026), it must meet three criteria:

1. **Generality**: the benefit is granted to all staff or to a category of staff.
2. **Constancy**: it is granted regularly, for example a bonus paid for several years.
3. **Fixity**: it is set according to precise rules, for example a calculation method set in advance with objective criteria.

Service-Public gives the example of a **year-end bonus** with a fixed amount or calculation method, granted to all staff every year for 5 years: it is a company custom.

An employee who claims a company custom must prove it exists. Any evidence is accepted: statements from other employees, payslips or notices displayed in the company. The information shown on a payslip is detailed in the article on [French payslip mandatory information]({{< relref "french-payslip-mandatory-information.md" >}}).

## What some collective agreements provide

The Code du travail numérique publishes, agreement by agreement, the bonuses provided for by industry texts. Four examples show how much sectors differ:

| Collective agreement | Bonus provided | Page updated on |
|---|---|---|
| Real estate (IDCC 1527) | 13th month equal to one month's total gross salary, paid on the basis of the December salary | 21 November 2023 |
| Banking (IDCC 2120) | Annual salary paid in 13 equal instalments, the 13th calculated pro rata and paid with the December salary, unless the company has different rules | 21 November 2023 |
| Fast food (IDCC 1501) | Annual bonus of €215 to €525 gross depending on length of service, from 1 year | 14 August 2024 |
| Telecommunications (IDCC 2148) | No bonus provided by the collective agreement | 11 January 2024 |

In fast food, the annual collective agreement bonus is added to other pay items, except bonuses that are in the nature of a 13th month or a seniority bonus. If such a bonus is lower overall than the agreement bonus, the company must top up the payment.

Where the collective agreement provides nothing, as in telecoms, a company agreement or the employment contract can still provide for a 13th month.

## Leaving during the year, absences and pro rata

The rights of an employee who joins or leaves during the year depend on the text that provides for the bonus. The agreements above show three different approaches:

- **Pro rata of time in the company**: in real estate, the bonus of employees leaving during the year is calculated according to their time in the company, on the basis of their last total gross monthly salary. Sick leave paid at 100% or 90% counts as time in the company.
- **Pro rata temporis**: in banking, the 13th instalment is calculated in proportion to the time spent in the company.
- **Presence on the payment date**: in fast food, the annual bonus is reserved for employees with at least 1 year of service who are on the payroll on the payment date. It is reduced in proportion to working time for part-time employees and for some absences (sick leave of more than 10 days, unjustified absences of more than 3 days, unpaid leave of more than 10 days).

When the bonus rests on the contract or on a company custom, their own rules apply. Hence the value, for the employer, of writing them down precisely.

## Social security contributions and minimum wage: how the bonus is treated

For the calculation of social security contributions, Service-Public states that bonuses, gratuities and all other cash benefits are part of pay. The 13th month and the year-end bonus are therefore subject to contributions like salary.

To check that pay reaches at least the Smic (French minimum wage), not all amounts count. According to the Service-Public page on the Smic (checked on 1 June 2026), holiday and year-end bonuses are not taken into account, unless they are paid in monthly instalments. The same applies to [overtime]({{< relref "overtime-pay-france-rules.md" >}}) premiums.

A 13th month paid in one go in December therefore cannot make up for a monthly salary below the Smic during the rest of the year.

## Removing or changing a year-end bonus

The employer's room for manoeuvre depends on the ground for the bonus:

- **Contractual bonus**: according to Service-Public, pay cannot be changed without the employee's consent. Removing a 13th month written into the contract therefore requires that consent.
- **Collective agreement bonus**: it applies as long as the collective agreement provides for it.
- **Company custom**: the employer can remove or change it without giving reasons, by following the termination procedure.

This procedure has three steps, even if the employer and employees agree:

1. Inform the CSE (social and economic committee, the French works council), where one exists, of the nature and effective date of the benefit removed or changed.
2. Inform each employee concerned individually, by ordinary or registered letter. A notice, a meeting or an internal memo is not enough.
3. Give sufficient notice to allow for possible negotiation.

Service-Public gives an example: to remove a 13th month paid in December, the employer must notify each employee in May and staff representatives in June. If the procedure is not followed, the custom continues to apply and the employee can claim it, if necessary before the employment tribunal (conseil de prud'hommes).
