> **In short:**
> 1. The **pre-employment medical check** in France is officially called the information and prevention visit (Vip). It takes place within a maximum of 3 months after the employee actually starts the job.
> 2. It must take place before starting for a night worker or an employee under 18, and within 2 months for an adult apprentice.
> 3. It takes place during working time with no loss of pay, and the employer covers travel. It is renewed at least every 5 years (3 years in some cases).
> 4. An employee already seen for an identical job in the past 5 years can be exempted. Jobs with particular risks fall under reinforced individual monitoring, with a fitness examination before hiring.

Every newly hired employee must have a **pre-employment medical check in France**, called the information and prevention visit. It is carried out by a professional from the occupational health and prevention service. The rules below apply to private-sector employees and come from the Service-Public guides on this visit (checked on 20 October 2025), on exemption (checked on 11 June 2026) and on reinforced individual monitoring (checked on 28 November 2025).

## What is the pre-employment medical check for?

The information and prevention visit applies to all employees of private companies, as well as those of public bodies employing staff under private law. It aims to:

- ask the employee about their health;
- inform them of the risks of their job;
- raise their awareness of the prevention measures to apply;
- identify whether their health or the risks they face require referral to the occupational physician;
- inform them of how their health will be monitored and of their right to request a visit with the occupational physician at any time.

An occupational health medical record is opened on this occasion. The occupational physician may request additional tests, paid for by the employer. They may not pass on medical information about the employee to the employer.

The visit is one of the [hiring formalities for an employee in France]({{< relref "hiring-formalities-employee-france.md" >}}), along with the pre-hiring declaration and the staff register.

## When must the pre-employment medical check be arranged?

The deadline depends on the employee's situation.

| Employee | Deadline for the visit |
|---|---|
| General case | 3 months maximum after actually starting the job |
| Night worker | Before starting the job |
| Employee or apprentice under 18 | Before starting the job |
| Adult apprentice | Within 2 months of hiring |
| Job with particular risks (reinforced monitoring) | Fitness examination before hiring |

The 3-month period runs from the actual start of the job, not from the signing of the contract. The rules specific to work-study contracts are covered in the article on [apprenticeship or professionalisation contracts]({{< relref "apprenticeship-or-professionalisation-contract-france.md" >}}).

## Who carries out the information and prevention visit?

The visit is not necessarily carried out by the occupational physician. It can be done by another occupational health professional, for example a collaborating physician, an occupational medicine intern or a nurse, if the employee does not face particular risks.

At the end of the visit, this professional may refer the employee to the occupational physician if they consider it necessary.

The visit is, however, carried out by the occupational physician when the employee is a recognised disabled worker, receives an invalidity pension or works at night.

## Is the employee paid during the visit?

Yes. Medical examinations take place during working time and pay is maintained. When they cannot take place during working hours, they are paid as actual working time.

Travel time and costs for these visits are also covered by the employer.

Service-Public gives the example of a night worker who has their visit during the day: with 1 hour of round-trip travel and a 45-minute visit, the employer pays them 1 hour 45 minutes of salary on top of their usual pay.

## Follow-up certificate and renewal of the visit

At the end of each visit, the occupational physician or health professional gives a follow-up certificate to the employee and the employer. It states the date of the visit and the date before which the next one will take place. It is kept in the occupational health medical record.

| Employee | Maximum period before the next visit |
|---|---|
| General case | 5 years after the first visit |
| Recognised disabled worker, invalidity pension holder or night worker | 3 years |

This period is set by the occupational physician, within these maximums. If unfitness is found, the physician may issue an unfitness notice.

Some HR software tracks these deadlines: this is the case, for example, of Kelio, whose plans include tracking medical visits and certifications from the second one onwards, as presented in the [Kelio review]({{< relref "kelio-review.md" >}}).

## When is an employee exempted from the pre-employment check?

A new employee can be exempted from a new visit if they had one recently, under three cumulative conditions:

1. they hold an identical job with equivalent exposure risks;
2. the health professional has their latest follow-up certificate or fitness notice;
3. no unfitness notice or job or working-time adjustment was issued by the occupational physician over the period.

| Employee | Previous visit taken into account |
|---|---|
| General case | Within the 5 years before hiring |
| Disabled, invalid or night worker, or one designated by the occupational physician | Within the 3 years before hiring |
| Temporary agency worker, before a new assignment | Certificate for the same job within the 2 years before hiring |

Exemption does not deprive the employee of an examination by the occupational physician: they can have one at their own request or the employer's.

## Reinforced individual monitoring for high-risk jobs

Employees exposed to particular risks for their health or safety do not have a simple information and prevention visit. They receive reinforced individual monitoring, which starts with a medical fitness examination by the occupational physician before hiring.

According to the Service-Public guide, the jobs concerned involve in particular exposure to:

- asbestos or lead, according to occupational exposure values;
- carcinogenic, mutagenic or reprotoxic agents;
- ionising radiation;
- biological agents of groups 3 and 4;
- hyperbaric environments;
- the risk of falling from height when assembling and dismantling scaffolding.

Monitoring also applies to jobs subject to a specific fitness examination, such as young people under 18 assigned to prohibited work under an exemption. The employer can add to the list, after consulting the occupational physician and the social and economic committee, justifying each job listed in writing.

The fitness examination is renewed at least every 4 years, and an intermediate visit takes place no later than 2 years after the visit with the occupational physician.
