> **In short:**
> 1. Under **job posting rules** in France, a posting must be dated, identify the employer and describe the job, the contract and the place of work.
> 2. It is written in French when the work is performed in France.
> 3. Mentions of sex, age or any discriminatory criterion are prohibited, apart from exceptions provided for by law.
> 4. A discriminatory posting carries 3 years' imprisonment and a 45,000 euro fine.

## Job posting: the employer's first showcase

A **job posting** is often the first contact between a company and a candidate. As such, it is part of employer branding. Its drafting is regulated: Entreprendre.Service-Public sets out what is expected and what is prohibited.

## Mandatory content

- A presentation of the company.
- The job title and a description of the main duties.
- The type of contract and its length (permanent, fixed-term, apprenticeship).
- The qualifications and experience required.
- The place of work.
- Contact details and how to apply.
- The publication date.

### Language

The posting is written in French when the work is performed in France or when the employer is French.

## Prohibited content

| Mention | Rule |
|---|---|
| Sex of the candidate | Prohibited, apart from exceptions (artists, models) |
| Age limit or range | Prohibited, unless a legal text requires it |
| False or misleading statements | Prohibited, whether about the job or the benefits offered |
| Discriminatory criteria | Prohibited (health, religion and other protected grounds) |

## Distributing the posting

The employer freely chooses its channels: company website, social networks, press, recruitment firms, job fairs. Using France Travail is not mandatory.

Before recruiting externally, certain employees must be informed of the vacancy, in particular part-time employees who want a full-time job and employees made redundant for economic reasons who have rehiring priority.

## Penalties

- False statements: 1 year's imprisonment and a 37,500 euro fine.
- Discrimination: 3 years' imprisonment and a 45,000 euro fine.

## What about pay?

The bill on [pay transparency]({{< relref "pay-transparency-eu-directive-france.md" >}}) provides that the employer discloses the proposed pay or a pay range during recruitment. This text has not yet been adopted.

## After publication

1. Limit the questions put to candidates to the [information an employer can ask a job candidate]({{< relref "information-employer-can-ask-job-candidate.md" >}}).
2. Keep a record of the published posting and its date.
3. Prepare the [hiring formalities]({{< relref "hiring-formalities-employee-france.md" >}}) as soon as the candidate is chosen.

## Pay, internal priorities and drafting support

### Pay is not yet a mandatory item

According to the Entreprendre.Service-Public guide, stating the salary and any additional pay in a job posting is not mandatory at present. The guide adds that this item could soon be required, because of the European directive on pay transparency.

### Priorities to respect before publishing

- **Priority access to the job**: this concerns for example a part-time employee who wants to work full-time.
- **Rehiring priority**: this benefits a former employee made redundant for economic reasons. It is mentioned in the dismissal letter and applies for one year from the end of the contract.

### Tools available

France Travail offers tools to help draft job postings. Using it to distribute them remains optional.
